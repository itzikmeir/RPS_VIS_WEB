Trust, but Verify: Calibrating Trust with Visual Explanations in AI-Guided Route Planning

Itzik Meir  |  Supervisors: Prof. Tsvi Kuflik & Dr. Joel Lanir

Department of Information Systems, University of Haifa

Abstract This report presents the full statistical analysis for a controlled within-subjects experiment examining how three visualization types — Stacked Bar, Radar Chart, and Heat Map — affect trust calibration and decision-making in an AI-guided, multi-criteria route planning task. The analysis addresses five research questions: (RQ1) the effect of visualization type on trust calibration score (TCS), response time, workload, confidence, route accuracy, and situational awareness; (RQ2a) moderation by task difficulty; (RQ2b) the effect of AI model quality (Optimal vs. Suboptimal) on workload, subjective trust, and behavioral calibration; (RQ3) the speed–comprehension trade-off; and (RQ4) individual-difference moderators. Repeated-measures ANOVA (parametric) and Friedman tests (non-parametric) are reported alongside Kendall's W, Cohen's d, and statistical power estimates. Results are interpreted with explicit notation of underpowered tests.
